## On Thoughts

Liu begins by arguing that to separate "Thoughts" (In this book, we can understand it as something like an ideology) and "Reality" into two distinctly separate boxes is a misconception.

While it is common to believe that thoughts are merely a reflection of reality (i.e., thoughts cannot exist without a reality to spark them), Liu argues the reverse is equally true: our reality would not exist in its current form without the thoughts that built it.

> [!Example]
> Let's say that you make an online purchase, your mind probably goes something like that:
>
> - If two items are equal, buy the cheapest one
> - If two items are the same price, buy the higher quality one.
>
> There is a logic that we can use to explain this action, which is to pick the highest "price-value" item.

This behavior seems entirely natural and "real," but it actually relies on a massive invisible framework of modern ideas. For this reality to exist, we first had to accept specific concepts: that human beings are fundamentally "rational actors" and "self-interested individuals."

Because our society adopted these ideas, we built an entire economic reality with e-commerce algorithms, consumer laws, and market pricing around them. Without these thoughts existing first, our modern shopping reality wouldn't just be misunderstood; it wouldn't exist at all. Thoughts aren't just descriptions of the world; they are the blueprints that construct it.

As such, it is good to understand thoughts, and how they shape and apply to our living reality.
